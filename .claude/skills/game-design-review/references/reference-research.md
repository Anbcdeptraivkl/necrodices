# Researching reference games

Use this workflow to explain a loop or borrow a mechanic. Record project-specific findings
in the project's research docs; a recommendation becomes a decision only when the project's
decision process adopts it.

## Scope the question

1. State the target player's promise and the decision the research should inform.
2. Name the reference game, mode, edition / DLC and version period. Do not combine several
   entries in a series into one imaginary ruleset.
3. Define the boundary: for an outside-combat loop, describe what battles consume and return
   without turning the task into a combat-system survey.

## Gather and classify evidence

- Prefer manuals, developer diaries, released patch notes and direct observations of a named
  build. Read the relevant passage rather than relying only on a search snippet when the
  full source is accessible.
- A developer diary explains the intended problem / solution at that time. It does not prove
  that every proposed feature shipped or remained unchanged.
- Check later official changes that could alter the conclusion. Keep base-game patches and
  paid expansion features separate.
- Mark player reports as experiences from a source / sample. A few forum posts do not show
  what most players enjoy; theme percentages without a known method are not preference data.
- When details cannot be verified, narrow the claim or leave it open. Do not reconstruct
  exact tutorial rewards, thresholds or algorithms from recollection.

## Reconstruct decisions and flows

For each subsystem, identify the recurring player choice, its prerequisites, its cost, its
result and the next decision it changes. Track how people, locations, time and resources
connect across the loop. Explain the mechanism that makes a feature valuable; a list of
systems is not a loop analysis.

Distinguish three outputs in the writing:

1. Sourced mechanics and explicitly stated developer intentions.
2. Inferences explaining relationships, tensions and possible failure modes.
3. Adaptations proposed for the target game, with the constraints they require.

The same label may represent a different rule in another game: a city-growth resource is not
automatically troop upkeep; an ally is not automatically a vassal. Check roles and semantics
before borrowing the implementation or its numbers.

## Close with a decision aid

Compare what to retain, adapt and leave out. Name the adaptation's opportunity cost and the
smallest observable effect that would justify it. Preserve the target project's scope and
currency / UI budget. If a prototype is proposed, state its question, pass / stop criteria
and timebox; do not report research as human-play validation.
